7. when $n=1$, $1^{3}+5\times1=6$, (so true for $n=1$)
    assume true for $n=k$ (assume true $k^{3}+5k=6p$, $p\in\mathbb{Z}$)

Note: Do not award $M1$ for statements such as 'let $n=k'$ or 'n=k is true'.
    'Assume $P_{k}$ is true' is accepted.
    Subsequent marks after this $M1$ are independent of this mark and can be awarded.

consider $n=k+1$ case
$(k+1)^{3}+5(k+1)\left(=k^{3}+3k^{2}+3k+1+5k+5=k^{3}+3k^{2}+8k+6\right)$
$=6p-5k+3k^{2}+8k+6$ OR $6p+3k^{2}+3k+6$ OR $(k^{3}+5k)+3k^{2}+3k+6$

EITHER
$=6p+6+3k(k+1)$
either $k$ or $k+1$ is even, therefore $3k(k+1)$ is a multiple of 6
OR
$=6p+3(k^{2}+k+2)$ or $6p+3(k^{2}+k)$ or equivalent
considers both $k$ is even and $k$ is odd, $(k^{2}+k+2)$ or $(k^{2}+k)$ is always even,
hence $3(k^{2}+k+2)$ or $3(k^{2}+k)$ is a multiple of 6

THEN
since true for $n=1$, and true for $n=k$ implies true for $n=k+1, \therefore$ true for $n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}$
