9. Assume  $ p^2 - 8q - 11 = 0 $,  $ (p, q \in \mathbb{Z}) $

Note: This M1 is dependent on the assumption of truth (implied by “assume” or “suppose that … is true”). Subsequent marks should be awarded independently.

EITHER

 $ p^2 = 8q + 11 \left( = 2(4q + 5) + 1 \right)  $ so  $ p^2 $ odd  $ \Rightarrow p $ odd

OR

p even  $ \Rightarrow p^2 - 8q = 11 $ even which is a contradiction so p is odd

Note: This R1 should be awarded for any valid reason to conclude that p must be odd.

THEN

 $ p = 2k + 1 \left( k \in \mathbb{Z} \right) $

 $ (2k + 1)^2 = 8q + 11 $

 $ 4k^2 + 4k + 1 = 8q + 11 $

 $ 4k^2 + 4k = 8q + 10 $

 $ 2k^2 + 2k = 4q + 5 $ or equivalent with one side odd and one side even

a contradiction as LHS is even and RHS is odd

Note: This R1 is dependent on all previous marks. Accept correct variations such as work based on p = 2k - 1.

therefore, if  $ p, q \in \mathbb{Z} $ then  $ p^2 - 8q - 11 \ne 0 $
